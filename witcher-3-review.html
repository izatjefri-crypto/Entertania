The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt Review

Reviewed: May 19, 2015
By KEZA MACDONALD
Posted: 4 months ago
45 comments
10 Masterpiece

The gold standard for open-world RPGs

The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt is a monumental achievement in game design. With its rich storytelling, morally complex quests, and a world that feels genuinely alive, it sets a benchmark that few games have matched. Geralt's final chapter is unforgettable.

— Keza MacDonald

Introduction

Nearly a decade after its release, The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt remains the benchmark against which all open-world RPGs are measured. CD Projekt Red's magnum opus is a game of staggering scope and depth, one that somehow manages to make its massive world feel intimate and personal. It's a game about monster hunting that's really about the monsters within us all, wrapped in one of the most beautifully realized game worlds ever created.

You play as Geralt of Rivia, a professional monster hunter searching for his adopted daughter Ciri, who is being pursued by the otherworldly Wild Hunt. The main quest is urgent and personal, but the game constantly encourages you to slow down, explore, and lose yourself in the countless stories scattered across the war-torn Northern Realms.

"The Witcher 3 isn't just a game—it's a world you can live in. Every corner holds a story, every character has a past, and every choice carries weight. It's storytelling at its finest."

A Living, Breathing World

The world of The Witcher 3 is its greatest achievement. Velen, Novigrad, and Skellige each feel like distinct, fully realized places with their own cultures, politics, and problems. Velen is a grim, war-torn no man's land. Novigrad is a bustling, morally complex city of crime and religion. Skellige is a harsh but beautiful island chain where honor and strength are everything.

The weather cycles, day-night cycles, and NPC routines make the world feel alive. Peasants go about their days, soldiers patrol, and monsters emerge at night. Random encounters and hidden secrets reward exploration at every turn. Even years later, players are still discovering new things in this world.

Quests That Matter

Where The Witcher 3 truly excels is in its quest design. Even the smallest side quest feels meaningful, with its own story, memorable characters, and moral choices that aren't always clear-cut. There are no "fetch quests" here—every contract tells a story, and the choices you make have consequences that ripple through the world.

The main quest is compelling, but the side content is where the game shines. The Bloody Baron questline alone is better written than most games' main stories. It deals with domestic abuse, loss, and redemption in ways that feel mature and nuanced. The game never tells you what the "right" choice is—it just presents the options and lets you live with the consequences.

Combat and Character Building

Combat in The Witcher 3 is a deliberate, tactical affair. You prepare for fights by studying your enemies, applying the right oils, and brewing appropriate potions. Signs (magic) give you options in combat, from crowd-controlling Igni to protective Quen. The combat has weight and requires timing and positioning.

The character progression system is deep without being overwhelming. You earn ability points to unlock new skills in trees for combat, signs, and alchemy. Mutagens add another layer of customization. You can build Geralt as a pure swordsman, a sign-spamming mage, an alchemy-focused tank, or a hybrid of all three.

The Voice of a Generation

Geralt's voice, provided by Doug Cockle, has become iconic. His gravelly, weary delivery perfectly captures a man who's seen too much but can't stop trying to do the right thing. The supporting cast is equally strong, with standout performances from Ciri, Yennefer, Triss, and the many memorable characters you meet along the way.

The dialogue is sharp, often funny, and always true to the characters. The game doesn't shy away from mature themes, handling them with the gravity they deserve. It's adult storytelling in the best sense of the word.

The Expansions

Hearts of Stone and Blood and Wine aren't just DLC—they're full-fledged expansions that rival the base game in quality. Hearts of Stone tells a compact, personal story about a man who made a deal with the devil. It's darker and more focused than the main game, with one of gaming's best villains, Gaunter O'Dimm.

Blood and Wine sends Geralt to the sun-drenched land of Toussaint, a stark contrast to the grim Northern Realms. It's a beautiful, vibrant region with its own problems, and it adds a new skill tree and mutations. It also provides a fitting, emotional conclusion to Geralt's story. Together, they add dozens of hours of content that's just as good as the main game.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • One of gaming's best-written stories and quests
  • A beautiful, living, believable open world
  • Memorable characters and voice acting
  • Meaningful choices with real consequences
  • Two of the best expansions ever made
  • Gwent is an entire game within a game
  • Hundreds of hours of quality content

Cons

  • Combat can feel floaty to some
  • Inventory management can be overwhelming
  • Roach's pathfinding has... issues
  • Some players find the opening area slow

Gwent

Gwent, the in-game card game, has become a phenomenon in its own right. It's a simple but deep game of building a deck and outsmarting your opponent. You can challenge nearly every NPC you meet, and collecting cards becomes an obsession. It's so popular it spawned its own standalone game. In The Witcher 3, it's a welcome distraction that can consume hours of your time.

The Next-Gen Update

The free next-gen update for PS5, Xbox Series X, and PC breathes new life into the game. It adds ray tracing, faster load times, improved textures, and a host of quality-of-life improvements. It also includes new quests inspired by the Netflix series and other goodies. It's the definitive way to experience the game.

Verdict

The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt is not just a great game—it's one of the greatest games ever made. Its combination of rich storytelling, memorable characters, and a world that begs to be explored has set a standard that few have matched. Nearly a decade later, it remains essential playing for anyone who loves RPGs, storytelling, or just great games. With two expansions that are masterpieces in their own right, it offers well over 100 hours of unforgettable content. Geralt's final chapter is a triumph. A perfect 10/10.
