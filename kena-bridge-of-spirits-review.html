Kena: Bridge of Spirits Review

Reviewed: September 21, 2021
By TOM MARKS
Posted: 3 months ago
27 comments
8.5 Great

A stunning debut from Ember Lab

Kena: Bridge of Spirits is a gorgeous action-adventure that blends Pixar-quality animation with surprisingly deep combat. The Rot are adorable, the world is beautiful, and the emotional story hits all the right notes. A remarkable first effort from a new studio.

— Tom Marks

Introduction

When Ember Lab first revealed Kena: Bridge of Spirits, it was easy to dismiss it as "that game with the cute little creatures." And yes, the Rot are absolutely adorable and will likely be the stars of your social media feeds. But beneath that Pixar-like exterior lies a surprisingly deep and challenging action-adventure game that serves as an impressive debut for a first-time studio.

You play as Kena, a young Spirit Guide searching for the sacred Mountain Shrine in a forgotten village cursed by darkness. Her staff serves as both a weapon and a tool, and she's accompanied by the Rot—tiny, dark creatures that are equal parts helpful companions and key mechanics for solving puzzles and defeating enemies.

"Kena: Bridge of Spirits proves that Ember Lab is a studio to watch. It's a gorgeous, emotional, and surprisingly challenging adventure that earns every one of its emotional beats."

The Rot Are Not Just Cute

Yes, the Rot are adorable. They'll follow you around, you can dress them up in little hats, and they make endearing sounds. But they're also integral to the gameplay. You collect Rot throughout your journey, and the more you have, the more abilities you unlock. They can move objects, turn corrupted areas back to life, stun enemies, and even transform into powerful forms during boss battles.

Finding and freeing Rot is one of the main collectible pursuits in the game, and it's always rewarding to add another one to your little army. Watching them march behind you never gets old.

Combat: Surprisingly Deep

Don't let the cute aesthetic fool you—Kena's combat has some real teeth. Your staff functions as both a melee weapon and a bow, and combat requires timing, dodging, and parrying. Enemies hit hard, and on harder difficulties, this game can be genuinely challenging. The parry window is tight, and later enemies have complex attack patterns that will test your skills.

The Rot add another layer to combat. You can command them to stun enemies, giving you openings for heavy attacks. As you progress, you'll unlock new Rot abilities like Rot Hammer, which deals massive damage, and Rot Arrow, which can target multiple enemies at once. Managing your Rot courage meter becomes essential in tougher fights.

A Gorgeous World

Kena is absolutely stunning. Ember Lab's background in animation is evident in every frame. The character models are expressive, the environments are lush and detailed, and the lighting creates a magical atmosphere. The village and its surrounding forests feel alive, with particles floating through the air and light streaming through trees.

The art direction draws from a blend of Eastern and Western influences, creating a unique aesthetic that feels both familiar and fresh. The ruined temples, overgrown villages, and mystical forests are a joy to explore.

Story and Characters

The story follows Kena as she helps restless spirits find peace. Each major spirit has a backstory that unfolds through beautifully animated cutscenes and environmental storytelling. The themes of grief, loss, and acceptance are handled with maturity, and the emotional moments land effectively.

Kena herself is a likable protagonist—determined, compassionate, and capable. The supporting characters, including the village children and the spirits you help, add depth to the world. The voice acting is solid across the board, with the Rot's adorable sounds being a particular highlight.

Puzzles and Exploration

The puzzles in Kena are clever without being frustrating. They often involve using the Rot to move objects, activate mechanisms, or clear corruption. Later puzzles combine multiple mechanics in satisfying ways. The world is semi-open, with paths that loop back on themselves and shortcuts that open up as you progress.

Exploration is rewarded with Rot, upgrade materials, and cosmetic hats for your little companions. The hats are a fun collectible, ranging from cute animal ears to silly fruit hats, and finding them all adds replayability.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Gorgeous Pixar-quality animation
  • The Rot are adorable and mechanically interesting
  • Surprisingly deep and challenging combat
  • Emotional story with mature themes
  • Clever puzzles and satisfying exploration
  • Excellent art direction and music

Cons

  • Camera can be problematic in tight spaces
  • Some difficulty spikes may frustrate
  • Relatively short (10-12 hours)
  • Limited enemy variety

Sound and Music

The soundtrack, composed by Ember Lab's own Jason Gallaty, is beautiful and evocative. It blends orchestral arrangements with traditional instruments to create a sound that's both epic and intimate. The main theme is memorable, and the music during emotional moments hits the right notes.

The sound design is equally impressive. The Rot's chirps, the swoosh of Kena's staff, and the ambient sounds of the forest all contribute to the immersion. The voice acting is solid, with Kena's actress delivering a warm and determined performance.

Performance and Value

On PS5, Kena runs at a smooth 60fps with fast load times. The DualSense controller is used well, with haptic feedback that makes you feel the impact of your attacks and the gentle rumble of the Rot. The game takes about 10-12 hours to complete, with additional time for collectibles and higher difficulty playthroughs.

At its price point, it offers good value for a game of this quality. The New Game+ mode and harder difficulties add replayability for those who want a challenge.

Verdict

Kena: Bridge of Spirits is a remarkable debut from Ember Lab. It's a game that wears its heart on its sleeve, delivering an emotional story wrapped in a beautiful package. The Rot are destined to become iconic, the combat has surprising depth, and the world is a joy to explore. While it has some minor flaws—the occasional camera issue and some difficulty spikes—they don't detract significantly from the overall experience. For fans of action-adventures like Zelda or Pixar movies, this is an easy recommendation. A great 8.5/10.
