God of War (2018) Review

Reviewed: April 20, 2018
By LUCY O'BRIEN
Posted: 2 months ago
30 comments
10 Masterpiece

A masterful reinvention that surpasses all expectations

God of War is a masterclass in game design. Santa Monica Studio has taken a beloved franchise and completely reinvented it, delivering an emotionally resonant story, visceral combat, and a world that begs to be explored. Kratos has never been better.

— Lucy O'Brien

Introduction

When God of War was first announced, many fans were skeptical. A older, wiser Kratos? A son? Norse mythology instead of Greek? And a over-the-shoulder camera? It seemed like Santa Monica Studio was taking enormous risks with a beloved franchise. But from the moment you take control of Kratos in God of War, it's clear that every single one of those risks paid off in spectacular fashion.

Set years after the events of God of War III, we find Kratos living in the realm of Midgard, trying to control his rage and live a quiet life with his young son, Atreus. But when his wife Faye passes away, she asks that her ashes be spread from the highest peak in all the realms. This simple request sets father and son on an epic journey through a stunningly realized Norse world, facing gods, monsters, and their own complicated relationship.

"God of War isn't just a great game—it's a landmark achievement that redefines what a franchise reinvention can be. It's emotional, brutal, beautiful, and unforgettable."

A New Kratos

The most remarkable achievement of God of War is its transformation of Kratos. Gone is the rage-fueled demigod who slaughtered entire pantheons. In his place is a father—still capable of incredible violence, but now burdened by regret and desperate to protect his son. Christopher Judge's performance as Kratos is nothing short of revelatory. He brings a gravitas and vulnerability to the character that makes every grunt and every rare smile feel earned.

The relationship between Kratos and Atreus is the heart of the game. Watching them grow from distant strangers to a true father-son duo over the course of the journey is beautifully handled. Atreus could have been an annoying sidekick, but instead he's a fully realized character who grows and learns alongside his father. Their banter during exploration, Kratos's gruff lessons, and the moments of genuine connection between them make the story deeply personal.

The Leviathan Axe

The Leviathan Axe is one of the most satisfying weapons ever designed in gaming. The ability to throw it and recall it with a press of a button never gets old. The weight of the axe, the sound it makes when it returns to your hand, the way it embeds in enemies—every aspect is perfect. Combat revolves around using the axe, your shield, and your bare fists in a dance of brutal efficiency.

As you progress, you unlock new runic attacks that add depth to combat. Atreus also joins the fight, shooting arrows at enemies and helping to stagger them. Later, you gain access to the Blades of Chaos, and the moment they return is one of the most thrilling in gaming history. The combat system is deep, rewarding, and always satisfying.

The World and Exploration

God of War isn't open world in the traditional sense, but it offers a semi-open hub design that encourages exploration. The Lake of Nine serves as a central hub, with new areas opening up as you gain abilities. The world is filled with secrets, side quests, and optional bosses that are just as challenging as the main story encounters.

The realms themselves are beautifully realized. From the snowy peaks of Midgard to the vibrant forests of Alfheim and the hellish landscape of Helheim, each area has a distinct visual identity. The art direction is stunning, and the attention to detail is remarkable. Every corner of the world feels hand-crafted and purposeful.

The Valkyries

The optional Valkyrie boss fights are some of the most challenging and rewarding encounters in any game. Each Valkyrie has unique attack patterns and requires mastery of the combat system to defeat. Sigrun, the Valkyrie Queen, is a true test of skill that will push even the most experienced players to their limits. Defeating her is one of gaming's greatest achievements.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Christopher Judge's performance as Kratos is iconic
  • The Leviathan Axe is the best weapon in gaming
  • Emotional, character-driven story
  • Stunning visuals and art direction
  • Deep, rewarding combat system
  • Challenging Valkyrie boss fights
  • No camera cuts - one continuous shot

Cons

  • Some players may find the RPG elements overwhelming
  • Limited enemy variety toward the end
  • Some side content feels repetitive

The One-Shot Camera

One of the most ambitious technical achievements of God of War is its presentation as a single, unbroken shot. From the opening menu to the final credits, there are no camera cuts. This creates an immersive experience that keeps you in the world at all times. The camera seamlessly moves behind Kratos during gameplay and glides into cinematic angles during cutscenes without ever breaking the illusion. It's a remarkable achievement that few other games have attempted.

Soundtrack and Audio

The soundtrack by Bear McCreary is absolutely phenomenal. It blends traditional Norse instruments with orchestral arrangements to create a score that is both epic and intimate. The main theme, "God of War," is instantly recognizable and perfectly captures the game's tone. The sound design is equally impressive—the crunch of the axe, the roar of enemies, and the subtle environmental sounds all contribute to the immersion.

Verdict

God of War is a masterpiece that transcends the action genre. It's a deeply personal story about fatherhood and legacy wrapped in one of the most satisfying combat systems ever created. Santa Monica Studio has taken a beloved franchise and reinvented it in every possible way, delivering an experience that is both fresh and respectful of its roots. Kratos's journey from rage-filled demigod to grieving father is one of the most compelling character arcs in gaming history. This is essential playing for anyone who appreciates exceptional game design, storytelling, or just wants to experience something truly special. A perfect 10/10.
